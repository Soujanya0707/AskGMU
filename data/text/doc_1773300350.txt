About the Conference
Simon Kuznets (1955) proposed that the relationship between economic growth
and inequality follows an inverted U-shaped pattern. Inequality tends to rise in
the early stages of development as economies move from agriculture to industry
and labour shifts from rural to urban sectors. With sustained growth and higher
income levels, this trend typically reverses due to structural transformation and
broader opportunities. Jeffrey Williamson extended this framework to regional
disparities, arguing that interregional inequality similarly widens initially but
narrows over time as migration, capital mobility, government intervention, and
interregional trade generate spillover effects. Convergence theories further
suggest that economies with comparable structural features tend to move
toward similar development outcomes.
Regional disparities constitute a major component of overall inequality and
often reflect deep-rooted structural disadvantages. Populations in economically
lagging regions frequently encounter limited opportunities and weaker life
prospects, which can erode social cohesion and heighten political tensions.
These concerns are particularly salient in developing countries where spatial
inequalities intersect with ethnic or resource-based divisions.
In India, Prof. Sukhamoy Chakravarti highlighted the spatial consequences of
shortcomings in planned development. Following liberalization, greater state-
level autonomy has produced divergent development trajectories, with some
states demonstrating stronger performance in growth, human development, and
poverty reduction. This conference aims to examine these evolving patterns of
regional inequality through multidisciplinary lenses.
